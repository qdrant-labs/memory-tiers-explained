Reading the upload numbers

Upload throughput is flat across presets (~0.2–0.3 batches/s) — the memory tier of the target collection barely moves total upload time, since ingestion is bottlenecked on write/indexing, not on where data ends up living for reads.

HS-Rec is the outlier: ~34% slower to upload than Cached (1,096.0s vs. 816.6s). It's the only preset combining Cold HNSW + pinned quantization — building and pinning the quantized index while the HNSW graph is cold appears to add real cost during ingestion, which is worth re-confirming at cloud scale since it directly affects re-index / restore time.

Disk footprint anomaly — flag before scaling up

⚠ HNSW inline storage + quantization inflates on-disk graph size ~470×

In the two presets that combine inline HNSW storage with quantization (Cold + SQ, HS-Rec), the HNSW graph's on-disk footprint jumps to ~162GB and ~161GB respectively — versus ~346–347MB in every other preset, including plain Cold (inline storage alone, no quantization). Total collection disk usage follows suit: ~172GB and ~172GB vs. ~9–11GB elsewhere, for the same 1.76M points.

Inline storage by itself (Cold, no quantization) does not cause this — its index disk size is normal (~346MB), matching Cached. The blow-up only appears when inline storage and quantization are combined, so it looks like a real interaction effect between the two settings rather than a property of either alone. collection-setup's source doesn't document this combination, so treat it as unverified until checked against Qdrant's own behavior.

Before running this at cloud scale: confirm whether this is expected Qdrant behavior for inline_storage + quantized collections (vs. a preallocation artifact that doesn't reflect real usage) — at 10–100× the point count, a 470× disk multiplier turns a modest dataset into a provisioning problem, and could make disk the actual bottleneck rather than search latency.

Takeaways

  • Cached is the latency baseline to beat. With everything in the page cache, p99 stays under 13.1ms and the worst query ever seen is 15.6ms — the tightest, most predictable distribution of the five presets, unsurprising since the whole 9.2GB working set fits comfortably in cache.
  • Quantization on the cached tier trades median for tail. Cached + SQ improves p50 from 10.7ms to 6.7ms (smaller vectors, cheaper scoring) but p99 balloons to 121.8ms and the max to 316.8ms. Its cache was still warming during the run (8.6→9.5GB) and it read 31.9GB from disk despite being nominally "cached" — some fraction of queries paid a genuine cold-page cost mid-benchmark.
  • Cold, unquantized, is the worst case by a wide margin. 5.4 qps, p99 of 582.5ms, and one query that took 30.5 seconds. Page cache grew from 429MB to 7.6GB during the benchmark, with 123.4GB of disk read to service 1,677 queries — an 18× read amplification over the actual data touched, consistent with repeated cold-page HNSW graph traversal rather than a one-time warm-up cost.
  • Quantization is what makes cold tolerable. Cold + SQ recovers throughput to 21.5 qps (4× over plain Cold) and cuts disk reads from 123.4GB to essentially zero (0.01GB) — most scoring happens against the small quantized vectors instead of paging in full-precision cold vectors. That drop in read volume is the real story, independent of the disk-footprint anomaly flagged above.
  • Pinning the quantized vectors (HS-Rec) buys the most predictable cold-tier tail. p99 of 38.0ms and a max of just 68.1ms — no multi-second spikes at all, unlike Cold + SQ's 9.0s max. The cost is upload time (slowest of all five, 1,096.0s) and losing payload storage entirely (this preset carries no payload / payload index), so it's a fit specifically for pure vector-search / recommendation workloads rather than general search.

For the cloud-scale run

  • Verify the disk-footprint anomaly first. If the ~470× HNSW inline+quantization multiplier holds at 10–100× the point count, provisioning for Cold + SQ / HS-Rec-style presets needs to budget disk very differently than the other three presets — check this before sizing cloud storage.
  • Separate cold-start cost from steady state. All cold-tier numbers here are first-run, cache-empty numbers. Consider a warm-up pass (discarded from the reported numbers) before measuring cold-tier presets at scale, so the report distinguishes "restore tax" from steady-state latency.
  • Watch vector IO read, not just latency. It was the clearest signal of what quantization actually buys under the cold tier (123.4GB → 0.01GB) — worth keeping as a tracked metric alongside qps/p99 at the larger scale.
